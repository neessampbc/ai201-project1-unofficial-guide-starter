Rating: 3.5/5 | Difficulty: 4/5 | Would take again: Yes
O'Brien is a heavy reader's professor. Expect 80-100 pages a week of primary sources and a textbook chapter. The exams are all essay — no multiple choice anywhere in this class. If you hate writing under time pressure, this isn't the section for you.

Rating: 2/5 | Difficulty: 4/5 | Would take again: No
The blue-book essay exams are intense. You get three prompts and have to write two full essays in 75 minutes. He grades on argument and use of specific evidence from the readings, so vague answers tank.

Rating: 4.5/5 | Difficulty: 4/5 | Would take again: Yes
Best history professor I've had. O'Brien's lectures are basically stories and he connects everything to the present. Yes the reading load is heavy, but if you take notes on the primary sources the essay exams are very doable.

Rating: 4/5 | Difficulty: 4/5 | Would take again: Yes
There's also a 10-page research paper due at the end. He scaffolds it with a proposal and an annotated bibliography so it never feels like a cliff. Start the reading early — falling behind is the #1 way people fail this class.

Rating: 3/5 | Difficulty: 4/5 | Would take again: Maybe
Fair but demanding. The reading is the real workload, not the lectures. O'Brien posts study questions before each essay exam that basically tell you the prompts, so use them to prepare your evidence ahead of time.
